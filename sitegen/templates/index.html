Chronic disease surveillance through standardized network exchange

A public health agency requests minimum-necessary, standardized hypertension and diabetes data from five simulated healthcare organizations through a simulated network, then deduplicates, maps codes and computes population measures. Every number on this page comes from synthetic data.

The problem

Chronic disease surveillance often depends on surveys, claims and one-off EHR extracts. Each request has its own portal, file layout and agreement, so providers repeat the work, patients seen at several organizations are double-counted, and local codes quietly drop out of analyses.

The idea

One purpose-checked request over a network, inspired by TEFCA's Public Health exchange purpose. Each organization returns standardized FHIR data, limited to what the use case needs, with every step audited. Exchange then adds what no single organization can see on its own.

What this lab shows

Purpose-based filtering (Public Health vs Treatment), Bulk Data-style export, record linkage with privacy-preserving tokens, terminology mapping, data quality by source, and measures adapted from CMS165 and CMS122.

Use case at a glance

Flow A · Public Health

The health department (PHA) sends T-PH with jurisdiction and date range → the broker checks role and purpose → bulk export from all nodes → a limited data set is returned (no names, MRNs, phone numbers or street addresses; PPRL tokens included) → the PHA normalizes, maps codes and deduplicates.

Flow B · Treatment (contrast)

A clinician sends T-TREAT for one patient → the full record with identifiers comes back from each organization holding a match. HIPAA minimum necessary doesn't apply to disclosures to a provider for treatment.
